# Sakinah Brand Direction

Updated: May 3, 2026

## Position

Sakinah is not:

- a therapy replacement
- a generic couples game app
- an AI relationship coach
- a social feed for couples

Sakinah is:

- a premium Muslim marriage ritual app
- private, calm, and spouse-specific
- structured around one small daily act of care
- emotionally mature, not playful by default

## Product Feeling

The app should feel:

- quiet
- premium
- reassuring
- deeply intentional
- faith-aware without becoming heavy-handed

The app should not feel:

- gamified for its own sake
- clinically therapeutic
- loud, slangy, or trend-chasing
- “startup cute”
- like a tool for random dating content

## Visual Direction

Keep the current teal, gold, and warm-paper base, but execute it with more restraint:

- Teal should carry trust and stillness, not tech energy
- Gold should feel ceremonial and editorial, not flashy
- Backgrounds should feel parchment-like and soft, not flat beige
- Dark mode should be warm and deep, not neon or blue-black

Recommended expression:

- Serif-forward titles
- Clean system body text
- Strong spacing and fewer decorative gestures
- Warm gradients used sparingly
- Minimal novelty animation

## Logo Relationship

The current crescent-and-star direction gives the app a useful symbolic anchor. The product should visually support that mark by leaning into:

- stillness over sparkle
- ritual over productivity
- intimacy over content overload

If the brand evolves later, the best direction is not a louder logo. It is a more refined system around the existing symbol:

- editorial typography
- calmer color hierarchy
- more disciplined icon usage
- more silence in layouts

## Tone Of Voice

Use copy that sounds:

- adult
- warm
- self-possessed
- clear

Avoid copy that sounds:

- needy
- over-excited
- too internet-native
- apologetic
- fake-spiritual

Preferred examples:

- “Begin with something real.”
- “Write one honest answer now.”
- “Invite your spouse into the same space when you’re ready.”
- “Private to your account and shared only with the spouse you invite.”

Avoid examples like:

- “Start growing together!!!”
- “Drop hints”
- “Spice things up” as a default brand voice
- “End-to-end encrypted” unless technically and legally true

## UX Principles

1. Show value before asking for money, but do it once.
2. Keep the app emotionally quiet after purchase.
3. Make spouse sharing feel natural, not like account setup work.
4. Reduce surfaces that feel decorative but do not help the relationship.
5. Use emptiness well. Premium products do not need to fill every corner.

## Visual Rules For Future Screens

- One strong message per screen
- One primary action per screen
- One warm accent, not many
- Use icons to support, not entertain
- Use animation to acknowledge progress, not to beg for attention

## Monetization Fit

Because Sakinah is now premium-first:

- the product must feel resolved from the first session
- onboarding must be calm but commercially direct
- the paywall context should feel earned, not manipulative
- post-purchase should immediately reinforce why the subscription matters

That is why the sequence should stay:

1. Setup
2. Save one real first answer
3. Mandatory hosted paywall
4. Main app
5. Calm spouse invite
